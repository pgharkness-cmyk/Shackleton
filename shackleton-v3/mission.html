Mission

Expedition to the Lunar South Pole

Shackleton Rover is a proposed British-led robotic expedition to the lunar south pole, focused on the exploration of permanently shadowed regions near Shackleton Crater.

The mission seeks to establish ground truth for water ice on the Moon: its distribution, accessibility, physical form, and relevance to future in-situ resource utilisation.

◐

Why the Moon?

The Moon is becoming a new frontier for science, infrastructure, and international coordination. A British surface mission would help focus national capability around a clear exploration goal.

✦

Why the South Pole?

The lunar south pole contains permanently shadowed regions where water ice may be preserved. These sites are scientifically valuable and strategically important for future exploration.

⚐

Why Shackleton?

Sir Ernest Shackleton travelled south in search of ice. This mission carries that echo into space: endurance, discovery, and responsible exploration at a new polar frontier.

Mission Objectives

The primary objective is to investigate volatile-bearing regolith within the lunar south polar region. The rover would travel from a sunlit landing area to selected shadowed exploration sites, chosen according to distance, risk, and scientific value.

At each site, the rover would deploy a shallow penetrator to examine the near-surface regolith. The mission would seek to determine whether water is present as adsorbed molecules, pore ice, ice-cement, or other volatile-bearing material.

The resulting data would help future mission designers understand how accessible lunar water ice may be, and whether it can support future propellant production, habitation, and wider solar-system exploration.

Exploration Philosophy

The Shackleton Rover concept is not only about science. It is also about how exploration should be conducted. The mission draws inspiration from the UK’s historic role in Antarctic exploration and the later development of Antarctica as a peaceful global commons.

The same spirit can guide lunar exploration: scientific cooperation, free navigation, environmental responsibility, and a fair balance between initiative, risk, and return.

Physical Presence

A meaningful role in lunar governance requires more than commentary from Earth. It requires a physical presence on the ground.

By placing British tracks on the lunar surface, Shackleton Rover would demonstrate intent, capability, and commitment at a time when the rules and norms of lunar activity are still being formed.

Mission Concept
1

Land near the lunar south pole

Arrive by CLPS or a similar commercial delivery service, targeting access to sunlit terrain near scientifically valuable shadowed regions.

2

Traverse to shadowed sites

Use a small rover platform to reach promising exploration locations that static landers cannot easily access.

3

Probe the regolith

Deploy a sonicated penetrator to investigate volatile-bearing material beneath the lunar surface.

4

Return knowledge to Earth

Provide data directly relevant to science, resource utilisation, mission design, and responsible lunar development.
