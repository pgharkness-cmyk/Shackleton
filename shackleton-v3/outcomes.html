A new world

The Shackleton Rover is proposed as an ambitious, inspirational, and strategic national endeavour.

The UK already has a growing presence at the moon. The descent engines for Blue Ghost were tested in Westcott, while the engines for Argonaut are being designed in Belfast. British teams already contribute strongly to lunar communications, regolith processing, and the good governance of remote frontiers through our long Antarctic heritage. We are ready to run a mission.

Our peers in Australia and Canada are already pursuing lunar rover programmes, and we possess an unusually broad cluster of relevant capabilities that can add further value to their endeavours. We can achieve first contact with water on the moon, not just as a scientific first, but as a route to the volatile prospecting and in-situ resource utilisation technologies that will be key to a future cislunar economy estimated to exceed $5bn in Europe alone within the next decade.

Shackleton will provide an opportunity to do something remarkable. Our project will demonstrate the value of optimism, competence, and ambition. With success, we will deliver an achievement that will boost national morale, solidify our capabilities for the decades to come, and create countless new careers in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics.
