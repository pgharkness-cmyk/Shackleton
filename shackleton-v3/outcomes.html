Shackleton Rover is intended not merely as a scientific mission, but as a strategic national endeavour. The UK was the third nation to land on Mars, and there is no technical reason why we should not also establish a meaningful physical presence on the Moon. Our peers in Australia and Canada are already pursuing lunar rover programmes, but the UK possesses an unusually broad cluster of relevant capabilities.

The descent engines for Blue Ghost were tested in Westcott, while the engines for Argonaut are being designed in Belfast. British teams contribute strongly to lunar communications, regolith processing, and the governance of remote frontiers through our long Antarctic heritage.

Shackleton Rover would strengthen this capability through a visible role in volatile prospecting and in-situ resource utilisation technologies. It also represents a practical first step towards participation in a future cislunar economy estimated to exceed $5bn in Europe alone within the next decade.

More broadly, the mission seeks to demonstrate optimism, competence, and peaceful exploration at the beginning of a new era beyond the Earth.
