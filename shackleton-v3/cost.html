The Investment

Shackleton Rover is conceived as a focused, lightweight lunar expedition designed to deliver meaningful scientific, technological, and strategic outcomes at comparatively low cost. The mission demonstrates how carefully targeted robotic exploration can provide substantial national capability without requiring the scale or expense of flagship international programmes.

The architecture deliberately prioritises simplicity, low mass, commercial delivery services, and the adaptation of existing technologies wherever practical. The rover would likely fly through NASA’s Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) programme: a framework through which commercial lunar landers deliver scientific and technological payloads to the lunar surface at significantly reduced cost compared with traditional government-led missions.

By concentrating on a tightly defined scientific objective, the mission seeks to maximise scientific and strategic return while remaining financially realistic. Several comparable lightweight rover concepts currently under development by peer nations, such as Australia and Canada, are not designed to deliver shallow subsurface investigation but do provide bounding for our price point.

With this in mind, the total proposed mission cost is approximately £22 million across the 2029/2030 funding period: less than one percent of the planned UK Space Agency budget during the same timeframe. In strategic terms, and in the context of the potential achievement, the mission represents a immense value for money.
