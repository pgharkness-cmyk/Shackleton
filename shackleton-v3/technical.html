Technical Overview

The Rover

The rover will have four-wheel skid steering, an extendable vertical mast for illumination and communications, and a vertical penetration payload. The chassis will have solar panels and contain a battery and phse-change pack sized to permit one-hour sorties to the dark followed by a ten-hour recovery window.

◻

Mobility

A compact four-wheel rover platform capable of traversing uneven regolith, shallow crater terrain, and transition zones between illuminated and shadowed regions.

⌁

Communications

The mission concept considers direct-to-Earth communications together with relay-assisted operations near difficult terrain and low-angle visibility conditions.

⬤

Subsurface Access

The rover carries a penetrator investigation system intended to examine volatile-bearing material beneath the lunar surface without conventional drilling.

Low Gravity Drilling Problem

Traditional rotary drilling systems rely heavily upon reaction mass and downward force. In low-gravity environments such as the Moon, generating sufficient force against the surface becomes significantly more difficult. This creates engineering challenges for lightweight robotic missions attempting to investigate subsurface material.

The Shackleton Rover concept therefore investigates alternatives to conventional drilling approaches. Rather than relying on continuous rotational excavation, the mission considers pulse-based penetration techniques capable of operating with reduced mass, lower reaction force requirements, and simplified mechanisms.

Pulse-Elevator Concept

The penetrator concept uses repeated impulse transfer rather than continuous rotational drilling. This approach may allow lightweight robotic systems to access shallow subsurface layers in low-gravity environments where traditional drilling methods become mechanically difficult.

The concept is intended primarily as a technology demonstration supporting future volatile prospecting missions and robotic subsurface investigation systems.
